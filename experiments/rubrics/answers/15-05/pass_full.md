1. **Why gen-2 collections slow down:** a full collection traverses every tracked object in generation 2. That population grows from 287,634 to 342,109 between collections, and the pause grows from 0.083 s to 0.146 s with it, so pause time is roughly proportional to the long-lived heap.
2. **What the growth indicates:** objects keep surviving into generation 2, so the application retains more long-lived objects over time (a leak or an unbounded cache). The rising "unreachable" counts (15k → 23k) mean reference cycles are being created steadily and are only reclaimed by these full collections (reference counting can't free cycles).
3. **Patterns:** (a) reference cycles: parent/child pointers, objects storing bound methods or closures of themselves, caught exceptions kept with their traceback frames; (b) unbounded caches: module-level dicts or `functools.lru_cache(maxsize=None)` keyed by request data; (c) event listeners or callbacks registered per request and never removed.
4. **Tuning:** `gc.freeze()` after startup so the long-lived baseline moves to the permanent generation and isn't rescanned; `gc.set_threshold(50_000, 20, 100)` (or similar) to make full collections rarer; `gc.disable()` around a latency-critical section followed by `gc.collect()` at an idle point. The real fix: break cycles (weakref, explicit cleanup) and bound the caches.
